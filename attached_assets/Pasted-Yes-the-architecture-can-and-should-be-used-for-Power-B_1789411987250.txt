Yes — the architecture can and should be used for Power Banks, Cables, and Car Chargers too. But I would not use one giant specification model where every product gets every field.

The right architecture is:

Common specification engine + category-specific specification modules.

Recommended structure

PRODUCT
│
├── Common Specifications
│   ├── Brand
│   ├── Dimensions
│   ├── Weight
│   ├── Warranty
│   └── Compatibility
│
├── Charger Specifications
│   ├── Max Power
│   ├── Ports
│   ├── PD / PPS / QC
│   ├── Power Distribution
│   ├── GaN
│   └── Protection
│
├── Power Bank Specifications
│   ├── Capacity (mAh)
│   ├── Battery Energy (Wh)
│   ├── Input
│   ├── Output
│   ├── Max Output
│   ├── Ports
│   ├── Recharge Time
│   ├── Wireless Charging
│   ├── Display
│   └── Pass-through Charging
│
├── Cable Specifications
│   ├── Connector A
│   ├── Connector B
│   ├── Length
│   ├── Max Power
│   ├── Max Data Speed
│   ├── USB Version
│   ├── E-marker
│   ├── Video Support
│   └── Material
│
└── Car Charger Specifications
    ├── Input Voltage
    ├── Max Output
    ├── Ports
    ├── PD / PPS / QC
    ├── Power Distribution
    ├── Car Compatibility
    └── Protection

Why this is better

A 100W USB-C cable needs completely different comparison data from a 20,000mAh power bank.

If you force both into the same fixed table, you'll eventually end up with something ugly like:

product
├── watt
├── mah
├── usb_speed
├── battery_capacity
├── gan
├── connector_a
├── connector_b
├── car_voltage
├── wireless
├── ...

That's exactly what we want to avoid.

Instead:

products
   ↓
category
   ↓
category specification definition
   ↓
product specification value

So the same engine powers all categories, while each category defines its own relevant specifications.

So yes: the previous prompt is reusable, but I would modify it before giving it to Replit to make it a true multi-category specification engine rather than a charger-focused system.
